Methods, assumptions & references

Cross-section flow. The axial momentum equation ρ∂u/∂t = G(t) + ∇·(μ∇u) is solved on a 65×65 grid with an implicit ADI scheme and cut-cell walls, so eccentric catheters, deposited clot and pulsatile (Womersley) effects are resolved, not assumed.

Blood. Carreau model (Cho & Kensey 1991): μ₀ = 56 mPa·s, μ∞ = 3.45 mPa·s, λ = 3.313 s, n = 0.3568, scaled by exp[2.31(Hct − 0.45)]. Density 1060 kg/m³. Newtonian option uses μ∞.

Flow coupling. Q = Q₀ / [(1 − f) + f·Rcath/R₀], where f is the resistance share of the segment. f = 1 reproduces the fixed-pressure model of Nifong & McDevitt (2011). Waveforms are typical adult shapes scaled to the mean flow.

Past the tip. The profile redevelops toward Poiseuille over L = D[0.6/(1 + 0.035Re) + 0.056Re] (Durst et al. 2005), leaving a slow wake behind the tip.

Infusion network. Lumen segments use Hagen–Poiseuille (Blasius above Re 2300). Each hole is a wall channel plus Sampson orifice resistance plus an inertial loss (K = 2.7), with pressure regain at each branch. Solved by shooting on tip pressure.

Withdrawal mode. Blood enters each hole as a point sink, so its capture radius is √(q/2πU) in a cross-flow U. Inlet loss K = 1.5. Merging flows inside the lumen lose pressure by ρ(v₂² − v₁²). A hole closer than 0.6 diameters to the vessel wall is sealed by suction. A fibrin sheath acts as a flap valve that blocks withdrawal but not infusion. Blood is damaged by shear in the holes and along the lumen.

Jets. Trajectory in cross-flow y/rd = 2.05(x/rd)^0.28 (Pratte & Baines 1967). Centreline decay u = 6.2·d·u₀/s. Wall impact shear τ = 0.44ρu²Re^−½ (Phares et al. 2000). Acute endothelial damage near 38 Pa (Fry 1968).

Blood damage. Giersiepen et al. (1990) power laws: hemolysis 3.62×10⁻⁵·t^0.785·τ^2.416 %, platelet LDH release 3.31×10⁻⁶·t^0.77·τ^3.075 %. Exposed blood flow from jet entrainment 0.32·q·x/d (Ricou & Spalding 1961), capped at the local blood flow. 5 L blood volume, Hb ≈ Hct/3.

Clot kinetics (semi-empirical). Coagulation propensity P(γ̇) = 1/[1 + (γ̇/30 s⁻¹)^2.5], reflecting the shear threshold of coagulation (Shen et al. 2008). Growth dδ/dt = J·H·P·(I + δ/δ₀) + platelet term − k(1 + τ/10 Pa)δ with J = 4 µm/h, δ₀ = 50 µm, k = 0.04 h⁻¹. Catheter surfaces add shear-independent protein adsorption. Holes wash out with flow velocity v/(v + 2 cm/s), and clot inside a hole is shielded from lysis (rate × 0.3). Calibrated to known trends: fibrin sheaths form within days, and stasis plus hypercoagulability propagates thrombus. Not validated against patient outcomes.

Thrombosis risk index. Virchow's triad: stasis (flow and wall-shear loss), endothelial injury (catheter-to-vessel ratio above 0.45 per Sharp et al. 2015, wall contact, jet impact) and hypercoagulability (patient, drugs, material, platelet activation). Index = 100(1 − e^(−3.5·S·E·H)). Use it to compare designs, not as a probability.

Limits. Rigid straight vessel, no valves or branches, catheter straight and parallel to the axis, cells drawn enlarged and not to scale. Educational model. Not for clinical decisions.
